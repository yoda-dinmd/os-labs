Lab 18 — Build a User-Level Thread Switch

Observe: What does thread_switch save and load, and why does ra make
execution continue in the other thread?

The first argument (a0) points to the outgoing thread's context, and the
second (a1) points to the incoming thread's context. thread_switch stores
ra (return address), sp (stack pointer), and s0 through s11 (the
callee-saved integer registers) into the outgoing context. It loads the
same 14 registers from the incoming context. The caller already follows
the RISC-V calling convention for caller-saved registers.

Calling thread_switch puts the resume address for the outgoing call in
ra. The switch saves that address and replaces it with the incoming
thread's ra. The ret instruction jumps to the address in the restored ra,
so it lands just after the incoming thread's earlier thread_switch call.
The restored sp puts that thread's own call frames back in use. For a
new thread, thread_create initializes ra to the entry function and sp to
the 16-byte-aligned top of its stack, so the first ret starts that function.
